Questions

Straight answers on price and process.

All eight questions
How do you price a project?+

In two contracts, on purpose. Pre-construction is its own engagement: we draw, estimate, reconcile the estimate to the drawings and buy out the trades. Only then is the construction agreement signed, cost-plus with an open book, convertible to a guaranteed maximum if you want one. The builder fee is the only profit in the job. It is never layered on labor and never applied to material you buy yourself.

What does pre-construction cost and what do I get?+

Pre-construction is paid on an hourly retainer agreed in writing before we start, billed against the hours actually worked. You get drawings coordinated with the architect, a line-item estimate reconciled to those drawings, a constructability review, a schedule, trade bids and the buyout. You keep all of that work product no matter what.

When do I know the real number?+

At Gate 3, buyout and budget lock. By then every major trade has priced the actual drawings and the budget is built from their numbers, not ours. That is the figure the construction agreement is signed against. Before that you see the estimate at Gate 2 and watch it move as decisions are made.

What if I am unhappy with the initial design?+

Say so. Gates 1 and 2 exist so the design is challenged early, while changing it costs hours rather than demolition. Because pre-construction is a separate engagement you are not locked into construction. If we part ways, you leave with the drawings, the estimate and the schedule, and no obligation to build with us.

Who is my point of contact?+

One person. Your Gilk lead walks every gate with you, from the first sketch to the last warranty walk, and sits in the standing weekly meeting with you and the architect.
